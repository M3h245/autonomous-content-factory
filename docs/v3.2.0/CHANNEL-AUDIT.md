## Read

1. **The problem is not views, it is audience.** A median AVP of 55.6 % is *fine* — these videos hold attention. But
   **six subscribers from sixteen published videos** means almost nobody comes back. This is the signature of a
   channel that is watched and then forgotten, not of a channel nobody watches.
2. **There is no channel promise.** Every short is an unrelated "why does X?" question. Nothing tells a viewer who
   finishes video N why they should watch video N+1. The factory's own research already derived this: all 36 runs are
   standalone everyday-science questions (`LONGFORM.md` §3).
3. **Views and retention are weakly coupled here.** The lowest-viewed video (168) has a median AVP; the highest-viewed
   (1945) has a median AVP; the best AVP belongs to a mid-table video. The algorithm is supplying reach; retention is
   not converting it into a following. More shorts of the same kind are unlikely to change that.
4. **This is the case for long-form — but as an audience tool, not a reach tool.** A 3–8 min deep-dive is where a
   viewer decides the channel is worth subscribing to. It will not fix view count on its own; it is the missing half of
   a funnel that currently has none.

## Consequence for the plan

- The long-form track stays at **one video, D7, no re-tuning** (already pre-registered). Nothing here changes the
  measurement rule — but it does change what "success" means: the number to watch is **subscribers and returning
  viewers**, with engaged-view share as the retention proxy.
- The channel-level gap (6 subs / 16 videos) is additionally a **packaging** problem, which is why P6 (cover taste)
  and P2 (steering reaching title/caption) were raised the same day.
