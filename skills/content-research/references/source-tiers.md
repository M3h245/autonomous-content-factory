# Source tiers and evidence rules
| Tier | What | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | government, intergovernmental, peer-reviewed journals, DOIs | *.gov, *.edu, who.int, europa.eu, nature.com, science.org, nejm.org |
| 2 | established newsrooms, reference works, reputable science media, preprints | reuters.com, apnews.com, bbc.com, britannica.com, scientificamerican.com, arxiv.org |
| 3 | everything else (blogs, company pages, aggregators) | — |
| 4 | social / user-generated | reddit, x.com, tiktok, youtube, quora, medium |

Risk: **high** = health, money, law, safety, or numbers viewers may act on → tier 1 or two independent domains.
**medium** = specific figures, dates, rankings → at least one tier ≤ 2. **low** = textbook knowledge → any credible source.
Search plan: mechanism · key numbers · common misconception · latest development (date-bounded) · counter-evidence.
Write claims in your own words; quotes only when wording matters, under 15 words, one per source.
If two good sources disagree, keep both views out of the script or present the disagreement explicitly.
